Groups WHO Disease Outbreak News reports into outbreaks and maps them by country. Case/death counts and country tags are extracted automatically from WHO's report text and may be incomplete or delayed — always verify against the linked source before using clinically. Open-source; PRs adding more sources welcome.
US state-level counts are the latest provisional week from CDC's National Notifiable Diseases Surveillance System (NNDSS), which is updated weekly and revised as reports come in — figures for the most recent week are usually incomplete and will rise. A blank state for a given disease means it isn't nationally notifiable there or data was unavailable that week, not that it had zero cases. Per-100,000 rates use Census Bureau 2024 state population estimates.
Brazil state-level data covers Severe Acute Respiratory Syndrome (SRAG) only — the condition InfoGripe tracks — not a general outbreak feed. Source: InfoGripe, a partnership between Fiocruz (PROCC), FGV (EMAp), and Brazil's Ministry of Health, published weekly. The most recent epidemiological week is provisional and will revise as more reports arrive. Risk levels ("intensidade") are Fiocruz's own population-adjusted classification, not raw case counts.
This mode shows all-cause mortality, not a specific disease — it was the only Latin American national dataset found (beyond Brazil) that was both structured and genuinely current; Chile's actual notifiable-disease surveillance (ENO) is roughly a year stale due to anonymization review. Source: DEIS/MINSAL (Chile's Ministry of Health), weekly. The most recent week's death count is almost always an undercount — deaths take time to register — so the "vs. baseline" percentage for the latest week is not a reliable trend signal; treat it as provisional.
England only (UKHSA's remit) — Scotland, Wales, and Northern Ireland have their own public health agencies not covered here. Source: the UKHSA data dashboard API. Each disease shown uses whichever of UKHSA's published indicators for it currently has the most recent data (test positivity, case counts, syndromic rates — the unit varies by disease, shown alongside each figure); a disease with no indicator updated in the last 90 days is left out entirely rather than shown with stale numbers.
Source: JIHS (Japan Institute for Health Security, formerly NIID), weekly IDWR provisional data across all 47 prefectures. Two kinds of diseases are mixed together here: "sentinel" diseases (common illnesses like flu, RSV, hand-foot-mouth disease) are counts from a fixed network of reporting clinics, not a national total — real case counts are higher. "All-case" diseases (measles, TB, syphilis, dengue, and other nationally notifiable conditions) are comprehensive counts. Figures are provisional and may be revised in JIHS's later confirmed annual reports. Per-100,000 rates (all-case diseases only) use 2020 census populations.
Source: the Robert Koch Institute's SurvStat database of notifiable-disease reports under Germany's Infection Protection Act (IfSG), by reporting week (when the local health office was notified, not symptom onset). The latest complete week is shown; it's provisional and rises as late reports arrive. Disease names appear exactly as SurvStat's English catalogue labels them — some only in German, some prefixed "(e)", a marker RKI doesn't document in its query service. Some diseases are notifiable only under individual states' own regulations ("Landesmeldeverordnung"), so a blank state means "not reported there", not zero cases. Per-100,000 rates use Destatis end-of-2023 state populations.
